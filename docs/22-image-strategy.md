## 1. Purpose

This document governs image planning, production, naming, storage, optimization, accessibility, rights, and page placement for the Ultimate Fleet GPS website.

It works with:

- 07-site-architecture.md for page families;

Images support the content. They must not invent a capability, market, employer promise, customer result, business relationship, or location.

## 2. Image Objectives

Images should:

1. Help candidates understand the physical and practical nature of installer work.
2. Make technical services easier for commercial buyers to recognize.
3. Establish a credible field-service context.
4. Give important pages a distinct visual identity.
5. Create calm space for readable headings and forms.
6. Improve engagement without delaying the primary action.
7. Support image search and local relevance through accurate metadata.
8. Remain useful when CSS, animation, or JavaScript is unavailable.

Images should not be used merely to fill an empty section or increase page length.

---

## 3. Visual Direction

### 3.1 Preferred look

Use premium, photorealistic editorial imagery with natural light, believable equipment, realistic work environments, and restrained composition. Images should feel observed rather than staged.

### 3.2 Field credibility

Show equipment and installation contexts accurately enough for a technically informed visitor to recognize the work. Avoid impossible cable paths, oversized tools, unsafe placement, fake screens, or generic technology imagery unrelated to mobile installation.

### 3.3 People

No-person imagery is the default when a scene communicates the work without a face. Gloved hands or partial figures may be used when they make the task clearer. Do not use identifiable employees, candidates, or customers without documented permission.

### 3.4 Brand restraint

Do not place logos, readable text, watermarks, fabricated vehicle branding, or invented manufacturer relationships into generated or sourced images. Brand colors may appear as subtle environmental accents, but imagery should not look artificially colorized.

### 3.5 Negative space

Hero prompts and selected section images must reserve calm space for text. The image brief should specify which side carries the copy:

- left text: keep the left 35–40 percent quiet;
- right form: keep the far right subdued and low-detail;
- centered text: keep the central field calm; and
- mobile crop: identify the subject that must remain visible.

## 4. Image Families

| Family | Primary audience | Typical placement |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment field | Candidates | Careers, roles, requirements, process |
| Active-job support | Candidates | Job detail and application context |
| Network pathway | Installer network contacts | Network page and no-opening fallback |
| Commercial service | Buyers | Services, industries, technology |
| Process and evidence | Candidates and buyers | How it works, proof, FAQs |
| Local context | Local visitors | Approved service-area pages |
| Brand and utility | All visitors | Homepage, About, Contact, legal |
| Editorial resource | Search visitors | Articles and guides |

Keep candidate imagery and commercial imagery related but not interchangeable when the audience needs a different signal.

## 5. Required Image Inventory

### 5.1 Launch foundation

- logo and approved brand mark;
- homepage hero image or image set;
- careers hero;
- current-openings or job-detail support image;
- role and requirements image;
- installer-network image or approved illustration;
- commercial hub or services image when commercial pages launch;
- contact or final-CTA background;
- favicon and social-share image; and
- accessible fallback treatment for every image-led hero.

### 5.2 Conditional inventory

- recruiting-market images;
- service-area images;
- industry images;
- technology-category images;
- approved customer or project evidence;
- article or guide thumbnails;
- process illustrations; and
- recruiting-platform graphics.

Do not create a complete image set for unapproved pages. Confirm page inventory and claims first.

## 6. Page Placement Strategy

### Homepage

Use one strong recruitment-first hero. A restrained multi-image hero may be used only if every frame has a distinct subject, loads responsibly, remains accessible, and does not rotate away from the primary CTA before the visitor can act.

Support the homepage with recruitment pathway imagery or cards, field-work/process imagery, discreet commercial context, approved evidence or trust imagery, and a final CTA image with calm copy space.

### Careers hub

Use an image that communicates practical installer work, equipment, field environments, or organized assignments. The image should support the employer and opportunity message without implying unverified benefits.

### Job detail

Use imagery sparingly. Job facts, requirements, and the application action are more important than decoration. One supporting image may reinforce the type of work if it is accurate to the opening.

### Evergreen role and requirements pages

Use instructional or field-context imagery. Avoid showing an unverified team, location, pay, vehicle, or schedule as though it were a role promise.

### Installer network

Use a neutral field or equipment image that supports future opportunities. Do not use urgency, “now hiring,” or active-vacancy visual treatment on the network page.

### Commercial pages

Use installation environments, equipment, fleet, facility, or project-context imagery that accurately reflects the approved scope. Do not use commercial images to imply customers, contracts, certifications, or coverage that are not verified.

### Resources

Use explanatory diagrams, close technical details, or editorial field scenes. Every image should help the reader understand the answer.

## 7. Hero Image Standards

### Technical

- preferred ratio: 16:9;
- source target: large enough for the widest approved crop;
- provide a mobile-safe crop or responsive focal point;
- reserve text space in the source composition;
- ensure foreground details do not compete with headings; and
- provide a solid-color fallback when contrast is uncertain.

### Composition brief

Every hero brief records page and audience, primary message, focal subject, copy side, safe crop area, lighting and setting, prohibited details, filename, alt-text intent, and intended breakpoint behavior.

### Hero carousel

If used, the carousel must have a meaningful reason to contain multiple frames, use distinct scenes rather than near-duplicates, include pause and previous/next controls, keep important information outside the rotating area, pause on focus and hover where appropriate, support reduced motion, and keep the CTA and H1 stable.

---

## 8. Service and Card Images

Service and card images should identify the subject quickly at small sizes. Favor one clear object or work context over a busy wide scene.

| Use | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Service card | 16:9 or 7:4 |
| Recruiting pathway card | 7:4 or 4:3 |
| Diagnostic detail | 4:3 |
| Resource thumbnail | 16:9 |
| Process icon or illustration | SVG or intrinsic ratio |

The image must remain legible when cropped and must not carry information that is absent from the card text.

---

## 9. Image Prompt Standard

AI-generated image briefs should include subject and technical context, audience and page purpose, environment and lighting, composition and negative-space direction, aspect ratio and approximate output size, realism requirements, and prohibited details. Filename and alt-text intent are recorded outside the generation prompt.

Prompts must exclude readable text, logos, watermarks, invented certifications or brand partnerships, identifiable addresses, unsafe installation practices, misleading likenesses, and details that make an unverified claim appear factual.

Generated images require human review. Generation does not establish technical accuracy or usage rights by itself.

---

## 10. Filename Standard

Use lowercase, descriptive, hyphen-separated filenames.

Recommended pattern:

ultimate-fleet-gps-[audience-or-service]-[subject]-[context]-[orientation].[ext]

Examples:

- ultimate-fleet-gps-careers-mobile-installer-equipment-field-work.webp
- ultimate-fleet-gps-current-openings-installer-work-vehicle-context.webp
- ultimate-fleet-gps-commercial-fleet-installation-equipment.webp
- ultimate-fleet-gps-installer-network-future-opportunities.webp

Rules:

- describe the visible subject;
- include a meaningful service, audience, or page context;
- do not keyword-stuff;
- do not include dates unless operationally meaningful;
- do not use camera-generated names;
- do not call an image “hero” unless the filename is also descriptive; and
- retain a stable filename when replacing an equivalent approved asset unless a migration is required.

---

## 11. Alt Text Standard

Alt text describes the image's useful information, not its SEO keywords.

### Informative image

Describe the subject and relevant context in one concise sentence.

Example: “Mobile installation equipment arranged beside a vehicle wiring access point.”

### Decorative image

Use empty alt text when the image adds atmosphere but no information. Do not omit the alt attribute.

### Functional image

Describe the destination or action when an image is the link or button label.

### Complex image

Provide short alt text plus nearby explanatory text or a long description when the image contains meaningful process or data detail.

Never use “image of,” keyword lists, claims not visible in the image, unapproved person identification, or embedded text as a replacement for HTML text.

---

## 12. Local and Geographic Imagery

Local imagery may support an approved market or service area, but an image alone cannot prove service coverage.

Rules:

- use recognizable local context only when accurate;
- avoid addresses, business signs, tenant names, and private property identifiers;
- do not imply an office, branch, or storefront unless verified;
- do not use landmarks to suggest work was performed there;
- keep geographic claims in page copy and structured data, not only in filenames; and
- review every image for accidental location misinformation.

---

## 13. Recruitment Platform and Social Assets

Where job postings or social recruitment campaigns use site imagery:

- keep role title and location in platform text, not baked into the image;
- use a clear, uncluttered crop;
- avoid claims that differ from the canonical job page;
- use the same approved role image across a campaign when consistency helps;
- preserve source attribution with campaign parameters; and
- remove or update assets when a job closes.

Platform artwork must not make network registration look like an active job application.

---

## 14. Storage and Organization

Recommended structure:

public/
  images/
    brand/
    careers/
    commercial/
    home/
    locations/
    resources/
    shared/

Keep source files outside the public output when they are not needed by the site. Store prompts, rights information, review notes, and final derivatives in the project asset workflow, not in page components.

Each approved asset should have a source or generation record, owner and usage status, page assignments, filename, alt-text draft, crop/focal-point notes, approval date, and replacement or expiration note where applicable.

---

## 15. Optimization and Delivery

Use the approved framework image helper and explicit dimensions. Optimize for the actual rendered slot, not only the original source.

Required practices:

- serve modern formats where supported;
- provide responsive widths;
- set width and height or aspect ratio to prevent layout shift;
- preload only the true above-the-fold hero;
- lazy-load below-the-fold images;
- avoid oversized card images;
- keep decorative background images from blocking content; and
- test compressed assets on mobile connections.

Do not load five large hero frames immediately just because a carousel exists. Numeric thresholds are defined in 24-performance-budget.md.

---

## 16. Background Images

Background images are appropriate for atmosphere and hero composition, not essential information.

Content must remain readable without the image. Provide a contrast overlay or solid fallback, use CSS background positioning intentionally, keep important text and labels in HTML, keep decorative images out of the accessibility tree, and verify crop behavior at mobile widths.

---

## 17. Rights, Privacy, and Approval

Before publication, confirm that the asset is owned, licensed, commissioned, or otherwise approved; identifiable people have permission; customer property and documents reveal no private information; marks are permitted and accurately contextualized; generated imagery does not imitate a real person or customer without approval; and the asset does not create an unsupported business claim.

Unapproved imagery remains unavailable for production routes.

---

## 18. Image QA Checklist

- [ ] Image subject matches the page and audience.
- [ ] Technical details are plausible and safe.
- [ ] No logo, watermark, readable text, address, or confidential information appears.
- [ ] Image crop preserves the focal subject on desktop and mobile.
- [ ] Negative space supports the actual overlay copy.
- [ ] Filename follows the naming standard.
- [ ] Alt text is concise, accurate, and non-keyword-stuffed.
- [ ] Decorative images use empty alt text.
- [ ] Image dimensions and responsive sizes are defined.
- [ ] Hero loading behavior is intentional.
- [ ] Contrast passes with the selected overlay.
- [ ] Rights and approval status are recorded.
- [ ] Closed or changed job assets are removed or reassigned.
- [ ] Build and visual QA pass at representative breakpoints.

---

## 19. Image Build Workflow

1. Confirm the page, audience, claim, and image role.
2. Select an existing approved asset where possible.
3. Write a constrained brief or generation prompt.
4. Produce or source candidate assets.
5. Review technical accuracy, privacy, composition, and rights.
6. Create the approved derivative and filename.
7. Add alt text and focal-point metadata.
8. Wire the asset through the page data model.
9. Test crop, contrast, loading, and static output.
10. Record the decision and publish only after approval.

Do not generate a large image library before the page inventory and content claims are stable.

---

## 20. Open Decisions

1. Final logo and brand asset package.
2. Approved font and image licensing sources.
3. Final careers and commercial launch page inventory.
4. Whether the homepage uses one hero image or an accessible carousel.
5. Which real employee, equipment, project, or facility photographs may be approved.
6. Whether a recurring image generation workflow needs its own metadata registry.
7. Final CDN and responsive image settings for Cloudflare Pages.
8. Whether social and recruitment platform assets need separate crops.

Unresolved choices must not be filled with invented people, locations, projects, logos, or outcomes.

---

## 21. Definition of Done

The image strategy is ready for implementation when every approved launch page has an image role or a deliberate text-first treatment; hero and card crops are defined; filenames and alt text are governed; assets have rights and approval records; recruitment and commercial imagery are distinguishable; images do not create unsupported claims; responsive and performance behavior is tested; and the public image library contains no unapproved or unnecessary assets.
